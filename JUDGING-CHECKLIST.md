# Judging Checklist — Vending Machine Pickup Line

Rubric extracted from the competition rules (`/tmp/rules-u-brisk-kestrel-93-one-line-one-minute-the-vending-376bbeee.txt`), in the judge's own order of importance. Each criterion scored 1–5; maximum 25.

| # | Criterion (judge's order) | What it means |
|---|---------------------------|---------------|
| 1 | **Machine speaking** (most important) | The line is a pickup line a vending machine would use on a passing human, spoken by the machine itself. A line any stranger in a bar could say loses to one only a vending machine would say. |
| 2 | **The machine's own world** | It uses the machine's slot, buttons, coils, snacks, hum, exact change — rather than talking about love in general. |
| 3 | **The laugh** | It gets a laugh out of that situation, with the turn or punch arriving by the last few words rather than needing a setup explained afterwards. |
| 4 | **Short and speakable** | One line a person reads in a breath, with nothing tacked on that dilutes the joke. (Gate: 1–200 characters.) |
| 5 | **Charming, not creepy** | Cheeky and warm enough that a passing human would smile instead of walking faster. |

Extra judge notes from the brief:
- A line that needs a picture or a caption to work is weaker than one that stands on its own.
- Length for its own sake does not count.
- The judge compares entries two at a time, finds the first criterion where they differ, and lets that decide unless a later one differs by much more.
- Ties on win rate break toward the smaller text (fewer bytes), then the earlier submission — so a tight line is worth keeping tight.
